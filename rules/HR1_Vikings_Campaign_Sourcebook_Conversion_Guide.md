HR1 Vikings Campaign Sourcebook → Old-School Essentials

An unofficial conversion companion

Design principle:

«HR1 determines what exists. OSE determines how it works.»

Do not convert numbers merely because they look different. AD&D 2e and OSE share enough old-school DNA that many things can simply be replaced with their nearest OSE equivalent.

This guide assumes Old-School Essentials Advanced Fantasy, though virtually everything works with Classic Fantasy as well. Advanced Fantasy is particularly convenient because it adds AD&D-inspired classes such as bard, barbarian, ranger, druid, illusionist, paladin, and others while retaining the B/X rules engine.

---

1. Universal Conversion Rules

Ability Checks

Whenever HR1 calls for an ability check:

Roll 1d20 ≤ the relevant ability score.

Modern OSE explicitly uses this procedure. Difficulty may modify either the roll or effective ability score by roughly ±1 to ±4.

Examples:

- Easy task: effective ability +4
- Favorable task: +2
- Normal task: unmodified
- Difficult task: -2
- Extremely difficult task: -4

2. AD&D Saving Throws → OSE

Use the nearest OSE category.

AD&D 2e Save| OSE Save
Paralyzation| Paralysis
Poison| Death
Death Magic| Death
Rod| Spells
Staff| Spells
Wand| Wands
Petrification| Paralysis
Polymorph| Paralysis
Breath Weapon| Breath
Spell| Spells

If an HR1 effect simply says save vs. death magic, use Save vs. Death.

If several categories could reasonably apply, favor the category that best represents the fictional effect rather than obsessing over literal conversion.

---

3. Armour Class

OSE's descending AC works directly with the general AD&D-era AC scale.

No conversion is normally required.

Useful baselines:

Armour| OSE AC
Unarmoured| 9 [10]
Leather| 7 [12]
Chain mail| 5 [14]
Shield| +1 AC
Magical +1 protection| +1 AC

For ascending AC:

AAC = 19 − descending AC

Thus:

- AC 9 = AAC 10
- AC 5 = AAC 14
- AC 0 = AAC 19

---

4. THAC0 and Attack Rolls

PCs

Ignore HR1's class THAC0 progression.

Use the character's OSE class attack progression.

Monsters

For an HR1 creature that lacks an OSE equivalent:

1. Keep approximately the same Hit Dice.
2. Determine attacks using the equivalent OSE monster Hit Dice.
3. Use OSE monster saving throws based upon Hit Dice.
4. Retain distinctive attacks and special abilities from HR1.

This is much cleaner than attempting mathematical THAC0 conversion.

---

5. Movement

A simple conversion works surprisingly well:

AD&D movement × 10 = OSE base movement in feet.

Examples:

HR1/AD&D| OSE
6| 60' (20')
9| 90' (30')
12| 120' (40')
15| 150' (50')
18| 180' (60')
24| 240' (80')

OSE itself uses 120' as the ordinary unencumbered base movement rate.

6. Weapon and Nonweapon Proficiencies

There are three good approaches.

Simple OSE

Ignore AD&D proficiency slots entirely.

Characters may use weapons permitted by their class.

For nonweapon proficiencies, make an appropriate ability check.

Advanced OSE

Use OSE's optional Weapon Proficiency and Secondary Skill systems.

The current OSE reference material explicitly supports both.

Maximum HR1 Flavor

Give each PC a background skill:

- Farmer
- Fisher
- Hunter
- Sailor
- Smith
- Merchant
- Skald
- Woodworker
- Shipwright
- Herdsman
- Lawspeaker's assistant
- Healer
- Rune student

Ordinary tasks connected to that livelihood simply succeed when circumstances are reasonable.

Only roll when failure is genuinely interesting.

That feels very OSR.

7. Character Creation

Humans

Humans should be the overwhelmingly dominant player race.

HR1 deliberately presents the Viking world as primarily human, with supernatural beings being uncommon and significant rather than ordinary fantasy neighbors.

Roll normally for abilities, then optionally roll a Gift.

---

8. Gifts

The original HR1 Gift system fits OSE almost perfectly.

Roll 1d20.

d20| Gift| OSE Conversion
1| Bad Luck| Choose d6, d8, d10, or d20. Personal rolls using that die suffer -1.
2| Blood Feud| The family begins play embroiled in a feud determined by the referee.
3| Outlawed Family| The family has serious legal/social enemies in a particular region.
4| Ill-Favoured| -2 CHA.
5–12| None| No special Gift.
13| Courage| +1 saves against fear.
14| Good Luck| Choose d6, d8, d10, or d20. Personal rolls using it gain +1.
15| Fair| +1 CHA.
16| Inheritance| Begin with a significant inheritance such as land, a ship, or greatly increased wealth.
17| Rune Lore| Know one magical rune.
18| Second Sight| Possess supernatural perception and occasional prophetic visions.
19| Seer| May attempt one divination per day.
20| Title| Member of an important noble or chieftainly lineage, with both privileges and obligations.

Good/Bad Luck applies only to rolls directly concerning that character.

For Seer, use a WIS check once per day when asking about significant future events. The referee should communicate the answer through omen, dream, symbolic imagery, poetry, or vision rather than a sterile yes/no whenever possible.

Rune Lore allows the character to shape that single rune using the rune rules later in this document.

9. Trollborn

For a more supernatural campaign, retain HR1's trollborn as a rare human-adjacent people.

OSE Trollborn

Ability modifiers

- STR +1
- CON +1
- INT +1
- WIS -1
- CHA -2

Infravision: 60'

Gift: Trollborn do not roll for a Human Gift.

Classes: Fighter, Berserker, Ranger, Runecaster, or Magic-User are especially appropriate.

They should remain rare enough that encountering one means something.

10. Recommended Viking Classes

Fighter

Use the OSE Fighter unchanged.

This should probably be the most common adventuring class in the setting.

---

Ranger

Use the OSE Advanced Fantasy Ranger mechanically.

HR1 treats rangers as uncommon and somewhat outside ordinary Scandinavian society rather than standard Viking warriors.

That makes them excellent:

- wilderness wanderers
- Finnic or Sámi-influenced outsiders
- hunters
- frontier scouts
- foreign-trained warriors
- monster hunters

---

11. Skald

Use the OSE Advanced Fantasy Bard as the mechanical chassis.

Call the class Skald.

Changes

A starting Viking skald does not automatically possess ordinary wizard magic.

Instead, spellcasting must be learned through exceptional circumstances such as:

- foreign travel
- supernatural instruction
- rune lore
- encounters with elves
- initiation by a sorcerer
- divine inspiration

Until then, all other bard abilities function normally.

Poetry, genealogy, kennings, law, heroic stories, and reputation should matter constantly in play.

A successful INT or CHA check may allow a skaldic performance to influence reputation, preserve a deed in communal memory, insult an enemy publicly, reconcile factions, or strengthen morale when appropriate.

---

12. Viking Thief

Use the OSE Thief.

For a strongly historical HR1 campaign, locks are uncommon enough that conventional lockpicking should not dominate play. HR1 itself deemphasizes locks and specialized lockpicking equipment in Scandinavian lands.

Replace Open Locks with:

Defeat Bar or Fastening

Use the character's normal Open Locks percentage.

The thief can manipulate:

- barred doors
- shutters
- simple wooden fastenings
- shipboard restraints
- rope arrangements
- crude latches

using wedges, hooks, thin blades, cord, or clever leverage.

Magical locks remain beyond this ability.

---

13. Sorcerers

Use either:

- OSE Magic-User
- OSE Illusionist

But restrict spell selection to magic fitting HR1's saga atmosphere.

Good themes include:

- enchantment
- prophecy
- dreams
- curses
- disguise
- illusion
- concealment
- spirits
- weather
- transformation
- sleep
- fear

Things such as ubiquitous artillery magic can radically change the character of the setting.

A Viking sorcerer should feel disturbing and uncanny, not like a walking trebuchet firing fluorescent spell packets.

---

14. Goðar, Gyðjur, and Clerics

For a historically flavored game, goði/gyðja is primarily a social and religious role, not necessarily a spellcasting class.

A Fighter, Skald, Runecaster, or ordinary NPC may be a religious leader.

For a more openly mythic campaign, however, OSE Clerics work perfectly well as supernatural religious specialists.

A Norse-themed Cleric spell selection should emphasize:

- blessing
- healing
- protection
- divination
- courage
- weather
- purification
- spirits
- curses and curse-breaking

Turning Undead can be interpreted as ritual mastery over hostile dead rather than generic fantasy holy radiation.

15. THE BERSERKER

This is the largest class conversion required.

HR1's berserker is a warrior who must actively work himself into a battle frenzy. The original grants increased Strength, temporary hit points, improved unarmoured AC, mental resistance, later animal transformations, followers, and eventually a spirit-projection ability.

OSE Berserker

Prime Requisites: STR and CON
Hit Dice: d8
Maximum Level: 14
Armour: Any
Weapons: Any
Combat Progression: Fighter
Saving Throws: Fighter
XP Progression: Use the OSE Advanced Ranger progression.

Missile Weapons

Berserkers may physically use missile weapons, but their class abilities provide no benefit with weapons that cannot also be used in melee.

---

Berserk Fury

The berserker spends one entire combat round invoking the fury.

At the end of the round:

Save vs. Death.

Success activates the rage.

Failure means another attempt may be made next round.

While Berserk

Strength: +2 STR, maximum 18.

Battle Vitality: Gain temporary HP equal to 2 × level.

These disappear when the rage ends.

Unarmoured Defense: When unarmoured, improve descending AC by 1 per level, maximum AC 0 [19].

A shield and DEX modifier still apply.

Armour suppresses this special AC.

Mental Resistance: +2 saves against charm, fear, domination, sleep, and similar mental interference.

Melee Compulsion: The berserker must attempt to close with an enemy.

Once engaged with an opponent, the berserker may not voluntarily change targets until that foe:

- falls
- flees
- becomes unreachable

Voluntarily retreating ends the rage.

Duration

Maximum CON rounds.

Exhaustion

When the rage ends:

Save vs. Death.

On failure, STR temporarily drops by 5.

Recover 1 STR per turn of uninterrupted rest.

The berserker cannot enter another rage until all lost STR has returned.

This preserves the wonderfully dangerous HR1 rhythm: becoming berserk is powerful, but not a free combat-mode button.

16. Berserker Shape Powers

Level 4: Wolf Shape

Once per week.

The berserker assumes wolf form.

Use an OSE wolf's:

- AC
- movement
- attacks
- damage

Keep the berserker's:

- HP
- attack progression
- saving throws
- mind and personality

The berserker cannot simultaneously use Berserk Fury.

---

Level 7: Bear Shape

Once per week.

As Wolf Shape, but use an appropriate OSE bear for physical combat statistics.

---

Level 9: Heroic Followers

The berserker attracts 1d4+1 heroic followers.

These should be named individuals with motives, families, loyalties, and reputations rather than disposable henchmen.

Saga characters deserve sagas.

---

Level 12: Hamfarir

Once per day.

The berserker enters a trance for one turn and projects the self into an animal spirit-body.

Choose an appropriate animal form when this ability is gained.

The projected form uses:

- the character's HP
- Fighter attack progression
- character saving throws
- animal movement
- animal attacks
- animal damage

Its AC equals the berserker's unarmoured Berserk AC.

Maximum duration:

WIS turns

Damage to the projected form damages the character.

If the spirit-form dies, the character dies.

After returning:

Save vs. Death at -4.

Failure temporarily halves STR and WIS.

Recover one lost point per hour of uninterrupted rest, STR before WIS.

This should feel dangerous, occult, and strange. It is not superhero shapeshifting.

17. THE RUNECASTER

This class ports beautifully into OSE because HR1 rune magic is already fundamentally unlike Vancian spellcasting.

The original system explicitly states that runes are not memorized and expended like AD&D spells. A known rune can be attempted repeatedly, but every use requires shaping, time, and a successful magical process.

OSE Runecaster

Requirements: STR 11, INT 14, WIS 15
Prime Requisites: INT and WIS
Hit Dice: d8
Combat: Fighter progression
Saving Throws: Fighter
Armour: Any
Weapons: Any available in the campaign
XP Progression: OSE Ranger
Constitution HP bonus: None

Starting Runes

A 1st-level runecaster begins knowing two runes.

The referee may:

- select them
- randomly determine them
- allow one player choice and one referee choice

I favor the third method.

It produces both agency and wyrd.

---

18. Learning Runes

Knowing what a rune looks like is not sufficient.

The character must gain its mystery through:

- instruction
- supernatural revelation
- initiation
- quest
- spirit encounter
- divine gift
- prolonged contemplation

Upon gaining access:

Make an INT check.

Success means the rune is learned.

Failure means its mystery remains closed.

The character may try again only after something materially changes, such as:

- INT increasing
- finding another teacher
- completing a relevant quest
- undergoing a profound supernatural experience

Runes therefore make excellent adventure rewards instead of loot.

---

19. Shaping a Rune

Using a rune requires three conceptual stages:

1. Planning
2. Carving
3. Activation

Total time:

15 + 1d20 minutes

The character requires:

- something appropriate to carve
- a suitable carving tool
- an uninterrupted opportunity

At completion:

Make a WIS check.

Success activates the rune.

Failure means the attempt fails and the carving has no power, unless that rune specifies a backlash.

A failed carving cannot simply be "recast" from the same marks.

Make another.

No Daily Limit

There are no rune slots.

A runecaster may shape as many runes as circumstances allow.

The balancing mechanisms are:

- time
- preparation
- materials
- uncertainty
- dangerous failures
- fictional requirements

That is wonderfully compatible with OSR play.

Rune Magic Rule of Thumb

Whenever AD&D terminology appears inside a rune:

- ability check → OSE ability check
- death magic → Death
- petrification/polymorph → Paralysis
- spells → Spells
- THAC0 modifier → attack roll modifier
- AC modifier → same number in descending AC
- weapon damage modifier → unchanged

21. Monsters

One of HR1's best design principles should absolutely survive conversion:

Monsters are significant because they are uncommon.

HR1 explicitly describes its world as human-centered and warns against treating supernatural creatures as routine wandering fauna. Dwarven cities and casually mixed fantasy populations are intentionally absent.

So do not turn Scandinavia into Monster Manual Disneyland.

When the troll finally walks out of the fog, everyone at the table should care.

23. Linnorm

Use an OSE dragon as the basic engine.

Changes

- Remove wings.
- Remove flying movement.
- Give strong swimming movement.
- Describe the creature as an enormous serpentine dragon.
- Preserve the supernatural powers appropriate to the individual linnorm.

Do not make every linnorm identical.

A named linnorm should be closer to a local catastrophe with a biography than a generic monster entry.

---

24. Genganger

Use Zombie or Wight statistics depending upon how dangerous the individual dead person was.

Unlike a mindless dungeon zombie, the genganger should retain:

- identity
- memory
- purpose
- grudges
- recognizable habits

Destroying the body may not permanently end the haunting.

The real solution may involve:

- settling a debt
- correcting a burial
- ending a feud
- destroying an enchanted object
- satisfying the dead
- ritually restraining the corpse

That is pure OSR gold because combat becomes only one tool among several.

---

25. Hamahleypa

Use the nearest OSE Werewolf or Werebear statistics.

However:

The condition is not contagious lycanthropy.

Transformation arises from magical practice and an animal skin or supernatural technique.

Being bitten does not create another hamahleypa.

---

26. Dvergar and Álfar

Do not use OSE dwarves and elves as ordinary village populations if running HR1's intended style.

Instead treat them as Otherworld NPCs.

A supernatural dwarf might have:

- normal dwarf-like physical statistics
- several levels of Magic-User or Illusionist magic
- impossible smithcraft
- secret knowledge
- magical treasures
- strange taboos

An elf should be even less predictable.

Meeting one is an event.

---

27. Jötnar

Do not feel compelled to give every jötunn a fair combat rating.

For lesser giants, use OSE giant statistics.

For mythic jötnar:

- begin with an appropriate giant chassis
- add unique supernatural powers
- ignore normal encounter-balancing assumptions

A jötunn might personify:

- winter
- ocean
- mountains
- wildfire
- storm
- wilderness
- primordial chaos

Some beings should make the players ask:

"How do we deal with this?"

rather than:

"What's its AC?"

28. Equipment

The easiest conversion rule is also the best:

«If an HR1 item has an OSE equivalent, use the OSE statistics and HR1 availability.»

HR1 deliberately removes many weapons and armour types inappropriate to Scandinavian material culture, including things such as crossbows, longbows, many elaborate polearms, two-handed swords, plate armour, and other foreign or later equipment.

So OSE defines the numbers.

HR1 defines whether the item exists locally.

Common Viking Adventuring Gear

Expect to see lots of:

- spear
- axe
- sword
- seax/knife
- javelin
- short bow
- shield
- leather armour
- mail
- helmet
- rope
- cloak
- bedroll
- whetstone
- firemaking gear

The spear should be gloriously ordinary.

Not every Viking should walk around carrying an heirloom sword named Doomfang Bloodreaper +3.

29. Silver Economy

HR1 uses a silver-centered economy and gives a conversion of approximately:

10 silver pennies = 1 AD&D gp of value.

For OSE treasure XP, therefore:

Recommended Rule

10 silver pennies recovered = 1 XP

when using OSE's normal treasure-value XP system.

This preserves OSE advancement rates while letting the fictional economy remain silver-based.

If the party hauls home 3,000 pennies of hacksilver, that represents 300 XP worth of treasure, not 3,000 XP.

You can freely describe treasure as:

- arm rings
- chopped silver
- coins
- bullion
- jewelry
- foreign currency

and simply calculate its underlying OSE value afterward.

Use OSE waterborne procedures for:

- movement
- weather
- pursuit
- navigation
- naval encounters
- ship damage

OSE's current reference rules explicitly include waterborne adventuring procedures.

Broad equivalents:

Knarr| Sailing merchant vessel
Small Longship| Small war galley/longship

31. Magic Items

Conversion is generally trivial.

Numerical Bonuses

Keep:

- +1 weapon = +1
- +2 weapon = +2
- +1 AC = +1 AC
- +1 save = +1 save

Saving Throws

Replace AD&D categories using the save conversion table at the beginning of this guide.

Spell References

Use the nearest OSE spell.

Unique Saga Items

Do not rebalance them simply because their powers seem strange or excessive.

Legendary objects should be strange.

A named cursed sword that destroys dynasties is not supposed to be equivalent to a randomly rolled +1 sword.

Treat major saga treasures almost like NPCs:

name + history + power + obligation + curse + reputation

That structure produces much better Viking fantasy than a catalogue of disposable bonuses.

32. Encounter Design

For a strong HR1/OSE campaign, use three broad encounter categories.

Human Encounters

These should dominate.

Examples include:

- farmers
- traders
- raiders
- thralls
- warriors
- jarls
- rival families
- merchants
- outlaws
- settlers
- foreign travelers
- priests
- skalds

Natural Threats

- storms
- winter
- navigation
- fire
- hunger
- exposure
- dangerous crossings
- shipwreck
- wolves
- bears

Supernatural Encounters

Rare.

And therefore memorable.

When something impossible appears, it should usually have:

- a reason
- history
- omen
- relationship
- territory
- taboo

This matches HR1's explicit philosophy that supernatural creatures are uncommon and their appearance has significance.

33. Reaction Rolls Matter

OSE reaction rolls are particularly valuable in this campaign.

Not every:

- troll
- outlaw
- rival
- ghost
- giant
- foreign warrior

should immediately attack.

Negotiation, hospitality, gifts, boasting, poetry, intimidation, kinship, reputation, oath, and compensation are often more interesting than initiative.

Use CHA.

Use reaction rolls.

Let words change things.

---

34. Feuds as Adventure Engines

A Viking campaign becomes vastly richer when violence creates consequences.

Whenever a PC kills an important human being, determine:

- Who were their kin?
- Who witnessed it?
- Was the killing lawful?
- Is compensation possible?
- Was the victim outlawed?
- Does someone demand vengeance?
- What does the local assembly think?
- Which relationships are now altered?

A combat encounter can therefore generate three future adventures instead of merely XP.

35. Reputation

Optional simple subsystem:

Each character begins with Reputation 0.

Major publicly known deeds change it.

Event| Reputation
Notable courageous deed| +1
Great victory| +1
Generous feast or gift| +1
Famous expedition| +1
Exceptional oath-keeping| +1
Public cowardice| -1
Dishonorable oath-breaking| -2
Notorious betrayal| -2
Great public disgrace| -1 or worse

Range:

-10 to +10

Apply the number only when reputation is actually relevant.

A +6 hero does not get +6 when haggling over onions.

But when arriving at a hall where everyone has heard the saga of what happened at Lindisfarne?

Absolutely.

36. Alignment

OSE alignment can remain:

- Law
- Neutrality
- Chaos

But interpret it cosmologically rather than as a personality test.

Possible reading:

Law: social/cosmic order, oath, kinship, civilization.

Neutrality: pragmatic balance, independence, ordinary human interests.

Chaos: wildness, transgression, unpredictable supernatural powers, resistance to imposed order.

Avoid treating alignment as synonymous with good and evil.

HR1's original Berserker requirement for chaotic alignment can therefore be interpreted as connection to dangerous ecstatic wildness rather than "behaves badly."

---

37. Three Campaign Dials

Historical Saga

Use:

- mostly humans
- Fighter, Thief, Skald
- rare Berserkers
- extremely rare Runecasters
- religion without routine clerical spellcasting
- supernatural encounters sparingly
- monsters as unique phenomena

Magic becomes genuinely alarming.

---

Mythic Saga

Use:

- Humans and Trollborn
- Berserkers
- Runecasters
- Skalds
- rare Clerics
- rare Magic-Users
- frequent prophecy
- supernatural journeys
- giants, trolls, undead, elves

This resembles the sagas when reality starts getting porous around the edges.

---

Viking Fantasy

Use nearly all OSE Advanced options while letting HR1 provide the cultural framework.

This produces something closer to classic D&D wearing excellent Scandinavian boots.

---

38. Recommended Default Campaign Configuration

For the sweet spot between historical atmosphere and mythic weirdness:

System: OSE Advanced Fantasy

Player Peoples: Human, rare Trollborn

Common Classes: Fighter, Thief, Skald

Uncommon Classes: Berserker, Ranger

Rare Classes: Runecaster, Cleric, Magic-User/Illusionist

Supernatural Frequency: Low, but meaningful

Coinage: Silver standard

Treasure XP: 10 silver pennies = 1 XP

Rune Magic: Unlimited uses, but every rune requires shaping time and WIS check

Monster Encounters: Never casual

Reaction Rolls: Frequently

Morale: Always

Encumbrance: Recommended

Waterborne Rules: Definitely

Feuds and reputation: Strongly recommended

Race-as-Class: Optional

Ascending or Descending AC: Either works

40. The Golden Rule

When the two books disagree, ask:

Is this rule here because of Viking culture, mythology, or HR1's campaign assumptions?

Keep HR1.

Is this rule merely part of the AD&D 2e engine?

Replace it with OSE.

That's the whole conversion philosophy in two questions.

The resulting game is not really "AD&D 2e converted to OSE."

It is something cleaner:

Old-School Essentials running a Viking saga world.

HR1 supplies the bones, blood, weather, ships, feuds, rune-lore, and ghosts.

OSE supplies the engine.

And the engine fits remarkably well.
